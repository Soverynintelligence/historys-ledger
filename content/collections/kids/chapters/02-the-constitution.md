# The Constitution — The Rules They Wrote Down
**Chapter 2 · Kids path · ages 10–12**  ·  *The paper is the boss*

> *"If men were angels, no government would be necessary."*
> — **James Madison**, *Federalist* No. 51, 1788

## After the promise, they needed rules

The Declaration said why they were breaking away. It did not say how to run the country every day.

Eleven years later they wrote a second paper: the **Constitution** (1787). This one is the rulebook. Who makes laws. Who carries them out. Who checks the rules. How you change the rules later.

A constitution is not a story. It is the boss — even over the people in charge. They put it in writing so anyone could point at it.

**Primary Source:** U.S. Constitution, 1787 — Articles I and IV.

## They did not trust anyone with all the power

The writers did not think leaders would always be kind. They thought people with power get greedy. So they split the job into three parts:

- **Congress** writes the laws.
- **The President** carries them out.
- **The courts** decide if the rules were followed.

No one part can do everything alone. That was on purpose.

James Madison said why, in plain words:

> *"If men were angels, no government would be necessary."*
> — **James Madison**, *Federalist* No. 51, 1788

He meant: people are not angels. So you build a government that can stop itself.

**Primary Source:** Federalist No. 51.

## The words they would not write

The Declaration had already said people are created equal. The Constitution is where they turned that into a machine — and where they also protected slavery.

Read the original Constitution. You will not find the word *slavery*. They used other words instead.

They counted enslaved people as **three fifths of all other Persons** when they decided how many seats a state got in Congress. That did not give those people three-fifths of a vote. It gave extra power to the states that held them.

They also wrote that Congress could not ban the slave trade until 1808. And they wrote that if a person escaped, they had to be sent back:

> *"Person held to Service or Labour."*
> — **U.S. Constitution**, 1787

**Primary Source:** U.S. Constitution, 1787 — Articles I and IV.

That was not an accident. They knew what they were doing well enough to hide the word. Both things are the founding: the rules that still run the country, and the bargains they made with slavery.

## They left a door

They also wrote **Article V**. That is the rule for changing the Constitution. It is hard on purpose — but it is possible.

That door is why later Americans could add the Bill of Rights (the first ten amendments — things like speech and religion), and why, much later, other amendments could try to make the Declaration’s promise true for more people.

The paper is still the boss. The paper can be changed. That is the machine they left running.

## What we know

✅ **They wrote rules even the government has to follow — and that government is still here.** A written constitution, power split three ways, and a way to change the rules without a king.

❌ **They also wrote slavery into those rules, and they knew it.** They hid the word, counted people they held as extra votes, and made free states send escaped people back.

**The takeaway:** The Declaration is the promise. The Constitution is the machine. You can read both. The promise is real. The bargains are real. What you make of that is yours.
