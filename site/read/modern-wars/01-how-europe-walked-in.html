IEstablished facts

What the evidence strongly supports.

What was achieved

The walk is in the documents, not in a mood. Ultimatum, reply, war telegram, two German declarations, a Belgian refusal, and a British speech to the Commons — dated, signed, and still readable. Europe did not "stumble." Cabinets wrote deadlines and then met them.

What it cost

The cost is written in the same ink as the march. Belgium was offered independence after the war if it opened the roads, and "the decision of arms" if it did not. Grey said the suffering would come whether Britain entered or stood aside. The German Chancellor called the occupation of Belgium contrary to international law in the same speech that asked the Reichstag to accept it. Necessity was the argument. The treaty was the thing it rode over.

Both columns are drawn from the same documents. Neither is our characterisation.

The Clock

The war did not arrive as a surprise. It arrived as paperwork with a deadline.

Vienna dated the instruction on 22 July 1914 and ordered it handed in Belgrade the afternoon of the 23rd. The note said the Sarajevo murder had been conceived at Belgrade. What We Learned

The honest ledger:

✅ The walk is in the documents, not in a mood. Ultimatum, reply, war telegram, two German declarations, a Belgian refusal, and a British speech to the Commons — dated, signed, and still readable. Europe did not "stumble." Cabinets wrote deadlines and then met them.

The German Chancellor called the occupation of Belgium contrary to international law in the same speech that asked the Reichstag to accept it. Necessity was the argument. The takeaway: 1914 is the setup so 1939 is not magic. The method is already here: notes with clocks, a small country's neutrality treated as a road, and a public admission that the law was being broken for a military object. The next chapter is the bill after twenty more years of that method. Here are the papers. You decide what "walked in" means.
